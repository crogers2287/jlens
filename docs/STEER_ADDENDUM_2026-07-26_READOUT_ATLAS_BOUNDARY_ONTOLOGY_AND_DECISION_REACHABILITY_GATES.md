## Primary external evidence

The public campaign `praxagent/jacobian-lens-research-202607a` preregistered two tests of Jacobian-lens depth-band ontology at immutable commit `98b1503e85876d7600aa9a56707afdf9b1cf4b6b`, amended only for mechanical layer-index alignment before any Test-B statistic at `f3f03c993f5597709439a5bc39bae57c8ca4f752`, and reported results at `911125f3ff92a27ad9ca7d0927b0b5c31b011f9c`.

Test B compared boundaries fitted to shared-vocabulary Jacobian readout maps against boundaries fitted to raw-activation linear-CKA maps. Five of eight preregistered models passed the activation-map sanity gate. The reported pooled normalized agreement was `0.501` against a random-segmentation null median of `0.459`, with `p = 0.608`; only two of five usable models beat their own null median. The point estimate therefore did not support boundary agreement.

Three models were excluded because their raw-activation CKA maps were effectively degenerate under the chosen instrument. The original positive branch also required six of eight models individually to beat their null medians, making that branch unreachable after three exclusions. The authors disclosed this decision-rule defect and bounded the interpretation to the surviving pooled criterion and point estimate rather than representing the frozen test as cleanly passed.

Test C asked whether corpus-matched lens boundaries predict layer-swap damage better than boundaries fitted on another corpus. The reported pooled matched coefficient was `-0.464`, opposite the preregistered positive direction. The pooled matched-minus-mismatched contrast was `-0.036`, approximately zero and also against the proposed rescue. One saturating cell failed its frozen dynamic-range gate and was excluded. The mechanical control returned exactly zero as required.

The external campaign consequently reports five designs across eleven models with no evidence that the fitted Jacobian depth bands partition computation, and direct evidence in the usable Test-B subset that the fitted lens boundaries do not track where raw activations reorganize under the chosen linear-CKA instrument. Its revised interpretation is that the atlas describes first-order output-readout geometry rather than demonstrated representational stages or computational phases.

The same campaign separately reported fit-budget convergence at commits `39230c030680a49bf66679b38db8eb8b7f849c1f` and `f32ec65879361283ad1261aab8df336022dee4bb`. Across 25 to 1,000 prompts on WikiText for GPT-2 small and Gemma-3-270M, fitted boundaries did not move and map distances remained within 1.9 times the same-corpus seed null. This supersedes the external-status statement in the prior fit-budget addendum that no fit-budget result existed. It does not supersede the binding requirement to establish convergence for the actual model, corpus, stage, estimator, and claimed object.

These are external author-supplied results. They have not been independently reproduced in jLens. Test B used one activation-similarity instrument, three models were instrument-degenerate, its positive-count clause became unreachable, Test C was preregistered as underpowered, and the fit-budget sweep covers two small dense models on one short-context corpus. Nothing establishes the same ontology, null result, or convergence behavior for large MoEs, Agents-A1, quantized execution, multimodal trajectories, long contexts, or production serving.

## Binding interpretation

The new evidence changes a scientific claim boundary not fully captured by corpus, fit-budget, or atlas-transfer controls alone:

> A fitted Jacobian depth map is a map of the admitted first-order readout operator over an admitted population. Its boundaries are readout-geometry boundaries unless a separate prospective experiment establishes correspondence with a named representational, computational, routing, memory, or behavioral-stage object.

The following objects are distinct and may not be renamed into one another:

1. Jacobian readout map or output-linearization map.
2. Raw-activation similarity map.
3. Hidden-state difference or trajectory map.
4. Information-flow or causal-influence map.
5. Attention, recurrent-state, cache, router, or expert-path map.
6. Optimization or training-dynamics phase.
7. Semantic or global-workspace phase.
8. Objective-error, recoverability, or policy-utility boundary.

A segmentation algorithm returning similar numbers of blocks does not make the underlying objects equivalent. Boundary coincidence is evidence only for the exact instruments and populations compared. Boundary disagreement does not make either map meaningless; it prohibits unqualified ontology transfer.

## Readout-atlas terminology gate

Absent separately admitted correspondence evidence, use terms such as:

- `Jacobian readout boundary`;
- `output-linearization band`;
- `corpus-conditioned readout atlas`;
- `first-order readout-geometry transition`.

Do not use unqualified terms such as:

- `computational stage`;
- `reasoning phase`;
- `representation transition`;
- `workspace ignition boundary`;
- `memory-write phase`;
- `router phase transition`;
- `semantic integration band`.

A readout atlas may remain scientifically useful as a compressed description of how output sensitivity changes with depth. Descriptive utility does not establish representational organization, causal partitioning, correctness monitoring, or control utility.

## Boundary-ontology admission gate

Any future claim assigning a non-readout ontology to fitted Jacobian bands must freeze the target object before evaluation and include compatible comparators from the relevant class.

For representational-stage claims, include where technically feasible:

- raw-activation CKA and at least one materially different representation-similarity metric;
- centered and normalized state differences;
- PCA or subspace-angle transitions;
- token-, position-, stage-, length-, corpus-, and checkpoint-matched controls;
- same-corpus and cross-corpus refit nulls;
- instrument-resolution and degeneracy diagnostics.

For computational-stage claims, include where technically feasible:

- prospectively frozen causal patching, ablation, or substitution measurements;
- matched within-layer and cross-layer controls;
- distance, position, residual-scale, and conditioning controls;
- direct output and downstream-state effects;
- random-segmentation and smooth-depth alternatives;
- dynamic-range, saturation, and recovery diagnostics.

For MoE routing-stage claims, include where technically feasible:

- router logits and probabilities;
- executed expert identities, ancestry, occupancy, transitions, and route entropy;
- nominal and functional expert diversity;
- route-regret and counterfactual equal-compute alternatives outside sealed evaluation;
- token-frequency, surprisal, trajectory-phase, and hidden-state comparators;
- route-conditioned downstream-state and objective-outcome effects.

For semantic-workspace or agent-stage claims, include where technically feasible:

- prefill and decode populations separately;
- visible trajectory, memory, compaction, requirement-ledger, and program-state baselines;
- external deterministic checks and verifiers;
- task-family-disjoint and answer-identity controls;
- objective correctness, recoverability, and irreversible-action boundaries;
- passive sealed incremental-value testing.

No single comparator is privileged. A null under one activation metric is scoped to that metric; a positive under one causal intervention is scoped to that intervention and target outcome.

## Instrument-resolution and undefined-comparison gate

Before interpreting agreement or disagreement between maps, each map-producing instrument must demonstrate usable resolution on the frozen population.

Freeze and report at least:

- diagonal and off-diagonal behavior;
- dynamic range and conditioning;
- finite-sample and same-population refit nulls;
- sensitivity to normalization, centering, token sampling, position sampling, and layer indexing;
- degeneracy thresholds chosen before outcome inspection;
- the exact disposition for failed or degenerate models.

If an activation, route, causal, or readout map is degenerate under its frozen instrument, the corresponding cross-map comparison is `undefined for that instrument and population`. It is not evidence of agreement, disagreement, absence of structure, or robustness.

A failed anchor or dynamic-range gate invalidates the affected comparison. Passing narrowly near a tolerance boundary must be reported with the actual margin, not only as `PASS`.

## Decision-reachability gate

Every preregistered decision table must remain capable of returning each advertised outcome after applying its own frozen exclusion and missingness rules.

Before execution, verify and record:

- minimum usable sample or model count for every branch;
- how failed, excluded, or undefined units affect each criterion;
- whether any branch becomes logically unreachable;
- whether the primary statistic remains identified after exclusions;
- the fallback interpretation if reachability fails.

If exclusions make a decision branch unreachable, the frozen test is structurally compromised. The result may still support a bounded statement through unaffected preregistered criteria, but it may not be described as cleanly passing the original decision rule.

Post-hoc removal of an unreachable clause is prohibited for confirmatory interpretation. A repaired rule requires a new preregistration and fresh data.

## Corpus-instability rescue restriction

Corpus-dependent boundary movement does not by itself rescue a failed causal or computational-stage hypothesis.

A claim that corpus mismatch attenuated a causal effect requires a prospectively frozen within-model comparison in which:

- the damage or objective measurement is held fixed;
- only the source of the segmentation or boundary labels changes;
- matched and mismatched arms use identical prompts, positions, layer pairs, runtime, and intervention magnitudes;
- random-segmentation and smooth-depth alternatives are included;
- dynamic-range and saturation gates are enforced;
- the matched-minus-mismatched contrast is reported directly.

If corpus-matched boundaries do not predict the named causal or objective effect better than mismatched boundaries, corpus dependence remains an estimator-property result. It does not establish computational relevance.

## Fit-budget result update and scope

The external 25-to-1,000-prompt sweep is evidence that fit budget was not a material map or boundary confound for two small dense models on the tested WikiText recipe relative to the campaign's same-corpus seed null.

It does not establish:

- convergence for Q35Q or either Agents-A1 checkpoint;
- convergence on agent, tool, code, multimodal, prefill, or decode populations;
- convergence of directions, monitor calibration, causal effects, or policy utility;
- that 1,000 prompts are universally sufficient;
- that corpus dependence is caused by fit-budget differences.

The existing fit-budget identity and convergence gate remains binding. Future jLens artifacts must establish convergence for every claimed object or remain explicitly scoped to the exact fit budget.

## Atlas-guided layer-selection restriction

Public or locally fitted Jacobian boundaries may not be used as privileged layer selectors for Agents-A1 capture, probes, interventions, route audits, or Jacobian studies merely because they form a visually stable atlas.

Before atlas-guided selection is admissible, demonstrate on training/development populations that it improves a frozen objective relative to:

- uniform or logarithmic layer coverage;
- random matched-size layer sets;
- raw-activation, direct-lens, logit, router, and trajectory-selected layers;
- smooth-depth models without discrete boundaries;
- complete feasible layer sweeps or prospectively justified subsets.

Until then, Jacobian atlas boundaries are descriptive readout coordinates. Agents-A1 studies must preserve enough layer coverage to detect effects outside the public or fitted bands.

## Monitor and intervention claim boundary

Readout-band reproducibility, activation-map agreement, corpus matching, causal damage association, or fit-budget convergence does not by itself establish:

- objective correctness or error awareness;
- planning, intent, deception, or evaluator awareness;
- semantic or global-workspace identity;
- recoverability, permission compliance, or tool safety;
- safe early exit, retry, repair, truncation, routing, steering, reward shaping, or production control.

Passive monitor value, causal intervention effects, and policy utility remain separate claims. Any readout, activation, route, or boundary score must show sealed incremental objective-outcome value beyond all cheaper compatible comparators before monitor language is admitted. Any intervention remains separately gated.

## Established by this correction

- Jacobian readout maps and activation-similarity maps are separate scientific objects.
- Fitted readout boundaries are not admitted as representational or computational stages without separate evidence.
- Cross-map comparisons require instrument-resolution and degeneracy gates.
- Preregistered decision branches must remain reachable after frozen exclusions.
- Corpus-dependent boundaries do not rescue a causal hypothesis without matched-versus-mismatched predictive value.
- The external two-model WikiText fit-budget sweep is now reported as converged over 25 to 1,000 prompts relative to its seed null, with strict scope.
- Atlas-guided layer selection requires prospective value over matched layer-selection alternatives.
- No privacy, sealed-data, verifier, provenance, derivative, resource, intervention, or production gate is weakened.
- Q35Q remains blocked.
